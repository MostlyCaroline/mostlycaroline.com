UX Case Study · Healthcare · Mobile App · KCL Programme

Private hospital booking system

Making healthcare booking feel human. A mobile booking experience for a private healthcare network, built with design agency Creative Navy as part of the King's College London Product Design programme.

In progress - research & synthesis complete. User flows and prototype coming in Module 3.
Industry Partner
Creative Navy
Client
Private Healthcare Network
My Role
Project Lead, Team 2
Platform
iOS Mobile

Situation

A brief built on an assumption

The client needed a unified mobile booking experience across its private healthcare network. The brief assumed the main blocker was speed - it asked us to get patients through a booking in under one minute.

Users

Adults seeking private healthcare, already anxious about a health concern before they've even opened the app.

Stakeholders

The specialists and clinics on the other end of each booking, who need enough context to prepare - and the hospital's admin team, who currently field the calls and questions the app is meant to absorb.

A booking isn't the end of the interaction. Patients still need to feel confident between confirming and walking through the door, and admin staff need fewer avoidable calls asking what happens next.

Insight

Speed wasn't the problem. Confidence was.

Seven user interviews and analysis of twelve direct, indirect and analogous competitors surfaced the same six themes again and again: uncertainty, trust, guidance, transparency, availability and cognitive effort. Speed was secondary to all of them.

  1. HMWreduce uncertainty before someone knows which specialist they need?
  2. HMWmake costs transparent before a patient commits?
  3. HMWkeep a human option visible if someone gets stuck?

Task

Find the real problem

As Project Lead for a team of seven, my job was to validate or challenge the speed-first assumption before anyone designed a single screen - and to turn whatever we found into a problem statement the whole team could design against.

  1. 01Discovery & Brief Review
  2. 02User Interviews
  3. 03Competitor & Analogous Analysis
  4. 04Affinity Mapping & Synthesis
  5. 05Problem Definition & Requirements

Action

Confidence, not speed

I directed the interviews, competitor analysis and a full affinity and empathy mapping synthesis workshop in Miro. Patients begin the booking process already worried about their health - what they actually needed was guidance to find the right specialist, transparent pricing, and visible access to a human if they got stuck.

"I'm already worried. I don't want this to make me more stressed."- User interview participant

Result

A reframed brief

The research reframed the design direction toward minimising cognitive effort rather than steps, and produced a prioritised set of requirements now driving the design phase. Leading the team also taught me the difference between project management and leadership: keeping tasks on schedule versus giving people direction when the brief itself is still being defined.
